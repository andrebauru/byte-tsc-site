A project in the planning stage

Earthquake information,
in a language you understand.

Quake RBM is a concept for a home device that delivers earthquake information through a screen and a voice — in Japanese, English and Portuguese. It is being planned as a complementary way for families to receive information when a phone is out of reach or language gets in the way.

Why this project exists

When an alert arrives, does it reach everyone?

An earthquake alert only helps if it is actually received and understood. The moments when it fails are closer to home than we tend to think.

🌙

When an alert arrives at night

Sound asleep, people may not hear a notification — and lose the seconds when it matters most.

🚪

When the phone is in another room

Charging in the hallway or left on a table, a phone can’t tell you what just happened — or what to do next.

🌐

When language is the barrier

For family members and visitors who don’t read Japanese, an official notification may as well not exist.

Quake RBM is a concept for filling these gaps with a second window on information in the home. Explore the project

The project

Quake RBM

A small device for the entryway or living room, designed to deliver earthquake information in clear language — a complementary home terminal alongside the smartphone.

Features under study

  • Information from authorized data services Planned Receiving earthquake-related information published by public sources, through authorized data services.
  • GNSS/GPS location, with a manual option Planned Location-aware information, with a manual region setting planned for when satellites aren’t reachable indoors.
  • Screen and voice interface in three languages Planned Both a visual display and spoken guidance, in Japanese, English and Portuguese.
  • FM radio reception Planned An information channel that does not depend on an internet connection.
  • Backup battery Planned An internal reserve power source, so core functions keep working during a blackout.
  • Alert LED light Planned Light-based notification, for when sound is hard to hear and as an additional visual cue.
  • Automatic alert detection from FM audio Experimental Detecting alerts automatically from broadcast audio is experimental: accuracy and false alarms are open questions we intend to study.
  • Taifu RBM (for typhoons) Future possibility A sibling project aimed at typhoons and storms — a possible future expansion beyond Quake.

Quake RBM is not a replacement for official disaster information. It is planned as a home device that complements the official channels. Alerts can arrive late — or not at all.

Development

How development is planned

The project is at the planning stage, before any prototype exists. This is the sequence we intend to follow.

  1. Requirements

    Define information sources, supported languages, and the screen and voice requirements — and how families would actually use it.

  2. Prototyping

    Build prototypes implementing the core features. The illustration on this page remains a concept until then.

  3. Reception and power testing

    Verify data and FM reception stability, and battery life, in conditions close to a real home.

  4. Accessibility improvements

    Keep refining volume, speech pace, screen legibility and controls.

  5. A possible evaluation batch

    Consider producing a small evaluation batch for real-world testing in homes.

As figures from the current proposal, the preliminary funding target is ¥3,000,000 and the estimated development time is about 10 months after funding is secured. Neither is a confirmed plan — both depend on securing support and investment.

FAQ

Questions and answers

When can I get one?

Quake RBM is a project in the planning stage — there is no release date at this point. Progress will be shared in the Development section.

Which languages will it support?

Japanese, English and Brazilian Portuguese are planned, on both screen and voice. Additional languages may be considered in the future.

What happens during a power or communication outage?

A backup battery and FM reception are planned, so some functions are intended to keep working without mains power or an internet connection. That said, the information available will be limited by the circumstances.

Will earthquake alerts always arrive in advance?

No. Depending on how alerts are distributed, they can arrive late — or not at all. Quake RBM is not a replacement for official information from public authorities and weather services; it is planned as a complementary home device. Always confirm evacuation and other decisions with official sources.
